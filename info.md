01. The Farm
Overview
Soans Farm is a working agricultural and horticultural estate located in the Moodbidri region of coastal Karnataka. Known for its combination of commercial agriculture, tropical fruit cultivation, spice crops, bamboo groves, and botanical diversity, the estate developed as a long-term agricultural project rather than a single-crop plantation.

The farm brings together cultivated plantations, fruit trees, plant collections, nursery activity, bamboo groves, and distinctive structures like its walking labyrinths. Its character is shaped by the interaction between productive agriculture and an extensive living collection of plants. It functions simultaneously as a working agricultural landscape, an experimental horticultural site, and a destination for agricultural observation and education.

History & Heritage
The documented history of the farm reaches back to the Basel Mission’s agricultural initiatives in the early 20th century, which sought to bring hilly and relatively uncultivated land under productive cultivation. Alfred Soans, an agricultural graduate who studied at the Allahabad Agricultural Institute, joined the project in the late 1920s and introduced a more systematic approach to crop selection. He established pineapple as the farm’s principal commercial produce, shaping its long-term identity.

Over subsequent decades, the estate expanded under the leadership of Dr. Livingston Chandramohan (L. C.) Soans, an agricultural scientist and botanist. His work focused on introducing unusual tropical fruit species, developing plant collections, cultivating bamboo varieties, and integrating traditional and alternative structures into the landscape. Following his passing in April 2023 at the age of 89, the estate’s ongoing management and interpretation have continued through the family and agricultural workers.

Philosophy
The farm’s approach is rooted in practical experimentation, crop diversity, and the productive use of land. Rather than treating agriculture as a single-purpose activity, Soans Farm relies on several core principles:

Combining crops with different growth patterns and uses.

Testing and cultivating fruit species from diverse geographic regions.

Maintaining a working relationship between agricultural production and plant study.

Preserving and developing plant diversity through nursery and horticultural work.

Adapting agriculture to local rainfall, soil, terrain, and water conditions.

Treating farming as both a livelihood and a field of knowledge.

02. The Land & Environment
Landscape & Climate
Situated in the rolling, hilly terrain of coastal Karnataka, Soans Farm experiences a tropical climate shaped by the southwest monsoon. The region receives substantial seasonal rainfall followed by a warmer, drier period. This climate supports dense vegetation and provides ideal growing conditions for tropical fruits, plantation crops, spices, bamboo, and ornamental plants.

The landscape is organized as a layered agricultural environment where different crops occupy various vertical levels. Open pineapple fields, tall plantation palms, shade-associated crops, fruit trees, and bamboo groves coexist to create a continuously varied estate.

Plant Life & Biodiversity
The plant life of Soans Farm includes commercial crops alongside plants maintained for botanical, ornamental, or educational interest. The estate's documented diversity encompasses food crops, plantation species, spice plants, fruit trees, palms, bamboo, ornamental plants, medicinal herbs, and exotic species.

While a formal inventory of fauna is not publicly available, the farm environment provides habitats for birds and other living organisms within its diverse vegetation structure, maintaining a large and varied cultivated plant landscape without formal ecological reserve status.

03. Agriculture & Cultivation
Key Crops & Plantations
Pineapple Plantation: The crop most closely associated with the estate, cultivated as a commercial produce within the mixed agricultural landscape. Fresh farm products and juice are part of the visitor experience.

Cocoa Plantation: Grown as a shade-tolerant understory plant beneath taller trees, producing pods directly from its trunk and larger branches.

Areca Nut Plantation: A major coastal Karnataka plantation crop whose slender palms and high crowns provide vertical structure and form part of multi-crop arrangements.

Bamboo Grove: Features multiple varieties—including giant bamboo, Burmese bamboo, yellow bamboo, Buddha’s belly bamboo, and small-leaf garden bamboo—forming shaded pathways and distinct landscape architecture.

Other Agricultural Activities
The farm's broader agricultural output includes a wide array of tropical fruits (such as rambutan, mangosteen, durian, langsat, longan, dragon fruit, jaboticaba, abiu, and miracle fruit), standard plantation crops (coconut, coffee, cashew, rubber), spices (pepper, vanilla, nutmeg, cinnamon, clove, and allspice), cereals, and nursery-propagated planting material. Current availability of produce and nursery plants varies by season.

04. Botanical Collection & Gardens
Development & Purpose
The botanical collection developed gradually through decades of agricultural and horticultural work, driven largely by L. C. Soans's interest in uncommon fruit species, bamboo, and plant science. It serves several purposes: supporting agricultural experimentation, preserving unusual tropical plants, providing fruit and plantation crops, developing nursery material, and serving as an informal setting for botanical learning.

Notable Plant Groups
The publicly documented collections include commercial fruit crops, exotic and uncommon fruit species, plantation crops, spices, bamboo, ornamental plants, medicinal herbs, and palms. Notable specimens include the diesel tree, macadamia, cola nut, Amherstia, Brownea, and various rare fruit trees. Plant names and historic lists require verification against current botanical references before formal use.

05. The Labyrinth
The farm features two prominent walking labyrinths: one based on a design associated with a French cathedral, and another based on the Cretan labyrinth tradition.

Unlike a maze, a labyrinth features a single continuous winding pathway leading to a centre and back out. Created in connection with L. C. Soans’s interest in ancient structures, energy concepts, and alternative traditions, these structures provide visitors with a quiet, deliberate space for walking, concentration, and reflection within the agricultural estate.

06. Visitor Experiences & Education
Exploring the Estate
A visit to Soans Farm offers an introduction to a working tropical agricultural estate rather than an urban botanical garden. Visitors encounter mixed plantations, fruit collections, bamboo groves, spice plantings, and nursery areas through walks and estate pathways.

Educational Value
The farm has long welcomed school and college students, agricultural learners, and travelers interested in tropical horticulture. It offers direct observation of crops, plantation structures, shade-based agriculture, and multi-crop farming methods.

07. Products & Estate Output
Fresh Fruit & Crops: Seasonal availability of estate-grown tropical fruits, coconuts, and agricultural produce.

Pineapple Juice: One of the most consistently associated products linking the farm's primary harvest to direct consumption.

Spices: Documented estate spices including pepper, vanilla, nutmeg, cinnamon, and clove.

Nursery Plants: Propagation material for fruit trees, ornamental plants, bamboo, herbs, and medicinal plants, subject to seasonal availability.
